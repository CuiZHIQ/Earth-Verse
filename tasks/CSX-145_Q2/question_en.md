# Super Typhoon Yagi Compound Impact Load Index

A hazard-impact analytics team is preparing a compact technical note on Super Typhoon Yagi's 1-7 September 2024 wind-and-rain impacts in northern Vietnam. The task is to decide whether the event reaches the top class of a Compound Impact Load Index, using the strongest supported wind and rain values together with reported human, housing, population, and public-service indicators.

Assign four component scores:

- Wind severity: 3 if the strongest supported wind value is at least 200 km/h or at least 125 mph; 2 if it is at least 150 km/h; otherwise 1.
- Rainfall severity: 3 if any supported reported event rainfall value is at least 250 mm; 2 if the strongest supported reported rainfall value is at least 100 mm; otherwise 1.
- Impact cascade: 3 if flooding or landslides occurred with at least 100 deaths and at least 10,000 damaged or destroyed houses; 2 if flooding or landslides occurred without both impact thresholds; otherwise 1.
- Public-service exposure load: 3 if the package exposure population estimate exceeds 1,000,000 and there are at least 75 critical or public-service amenity features; 2 if only one threshold is met; otherwise 1. Count hospitals, police facilities, fire stations, shelters, and schools.

Sum the four components. Interpret 10-12 as extreme compound wind-rain impact load, 7-9 as elevated mixed-hazard impact load, and 4-6 as limited compound impact signal.

Write 1-2 paragraphs. Include the total index score, all four component scores, the final load class, and a concise explanation tying the wind and rain thresholds to the reported flood or landslide, death, house-damage, population, and public-service counts. Avoid claiming asset-level outage, flood-depth, or building-level mapping unless the metrics directly support it.
